Speed is described as how **fast an object is moving**, and it's SI unit is $m/s$. 

- Speed is calculated using the formula $speed = \frac{distance}{time}$
- Unlike velocity, which is a [[Types of Quantities#Vector Quantities|vector quantity]]. Speed is a [[Types of Quantities#Scalar Quantities|scalar quantity]], meaning it has no direction, only magnitude.

---

There are also multiple different types of speed, like:

- **Average Speed**: Calculated by dividing the total distance by total time taken.
- **Instantaneous Speed**: The average speed in one specific instant. If your speedometer shows $60km/h$ on that time period, your instantaneous speed is $60km/h$.
  
- **Uniform Speed**: The speed remains constant over time. If you sprint the first lap in 20s and the next in 20s, that is uniform speed.
- **Non-Uniform Speed**: The complete opposite of uniform speed. Most real life motion is non-uniform.

## Relative Speed

Relative speed describes the concept of how an object's speed can be perceived differently based on a specific reference frame, which may also be moving.

I think relative speed is easier to grasp with an example, so lets give you one.

Let's say an object was moving at $90m/s$ and you were also moving at $90m/s$ in the same direction.
It would seem like that object was stationary, even when it was moving at a fast pace.
This means the object's speed relative to you, is $0m/s$

However, another stationary observer of the object would say it is moving at $90m/s$.

TLDR: Your perception of another object's speed is relative to your speed as well, or your reference frame.
