> [!ABSTRACT] Definition
>
> **Matter** is anything that has mass and takes up space (volume). Matter can shift between different **States of Matter** based on thermal energy (temperature) and pressure, or transform entirely through chemical reactions.

---

## The 5 States of Matter

States of matter are distinct forms that different phases of matter take on. They are determined by how much kinetic energy the particles have and how tightly they are bound together.

### 1. Solid

Low energy, highly rigid state of matter. Particles are tightly packed in fixed arrangements, they don't move around, they only vibrate in place as the intermolecular forces are strong.

Solids have a fixed volume, fixed shape, and due to that it is unable to be compressed or flow.

### 2. Liquid

Medium energy, fluid state of matter. Particles now have enough energy to break their rigid structures and slide past one another, but they're still close enough to not be a gas.

Liquids have fixed volume, but they can change shape, and it is due to that they are able to be compressed (sorta) and flow.

### 3. Gas

High energy, completely free state of matter. These things have high enough kinetic energy to overcome intermolecular attractions.

Gases are unpredictable, they can change shape, volume (expands to fill any container due to diffusion), and it is why they are highly compressible and have a low density.

### 4. Plasma

Extreme energy, super-heated, ionized gas. Atoms are moving so violently that their outer electrons are ripped completely off, which leaves behind a lot of free electrons and positive ions. An example of plasma is fire.

Plasmas have the same properties as a gas except they can conduct electricity and respond strongly to magnetic fields.

### 5. Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC)

The lowest possible energy state of matter, occurring only a fraction of a degree above **Absolute Zero** ($-273.15^\circ\text{C}$ or $0^\circ K$). Molecular motion almost completely stops Individual atoms lose their unique properties and clump into a single quantum atom that behaves as a single unit.

They have a fixed shape and fixed volume, similar to a normal solid.

> [!ABSTRACT] Absolute Zero
>
> Absolute Zero is the phenomenon that happens when a system reaches $0^\circ K$ or $-273.15^\circ\text{C}$. In this state:
>
> - The atoms freeze completely in place, there is no molecular motion.
> - As we noted before, atoms lose their individual identities and clump together into a single quantum atom. They start acting like one giant particle instead of millions of individual ones.
> - They will become superconductors (no electrical resistance), and fluids will become superfluids (no viscosity aka no fluid friction)

## Types of Changes

#### Physical Changes

A physical change alters the form, size, or state of a substance, but **does not change its chemical composition**. The molecules remain exactly the same. These types of changes are usually easy to reverse.

- **Examples:**
    - Crushing a soda can (it's still aluminum).
    - Melting ice (it changes state, but the molecules are still $H_2O$).
    - Dissolving sugar in water (the sugar is still there, just mixed evenly).
    - Chopping wood into sawdust.

#### Chemical Changes

A chemical change (or **chemical reaction**) occurs when substances combine or break apart to **form entirely new chemical substances** with completely different properties. Chemical bonds are broken and formed. These are difficult or impossible to reverse without another chemical reaction.

- **Key Trait:** Difficult or impossible to reverse without another chemical reaction.
- **Examples:**
    - Rusting iron ($Fe$ reacts with oxygen to create iron oxide, $Fe_2O_3$).
    - Burning wood (combustion turns cellulose into ash, carbon dioxide, and water vapor).
    - Baking a cake (heat permanently alters the proteins and chemical bonds in the ingredients).
    - Digesting food.

> [!tip] 4 Signs a Chemical Change Has Occurred
> If you notice these signs, a chemical reaction is likely happening:
>
> 1. **Unexpected Color Change:** (e.g., silver metal turning red-brown as it rusts).
> 2. **Temperature / Light Change:** Energy is released or absorbed (e.g., a fire getting hot or a glowstick lighting up).
> 3. **Gas Production:** Bubbles form without you boiling it (e.g., dropping antacid tablets into water).
> 4. **Precipitate Formation:** Two liquids are mixed together and a solid suddenly forms and falls to the bottom.
