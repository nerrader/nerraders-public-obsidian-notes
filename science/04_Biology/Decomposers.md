> [!ABSTRACT]
>
> Some microorganisms like bacteria and fungi are decomposers, they break down dead organisms and animal waste into simpler [[Organic and Inorganic Materials#Inorganic|inorganic]] materials in a process called decay. This allows substances and wastes in the dead organisms to be recycled, making nutrients available for primary producers.

There are **technically** four main decomposer types: bacteria, [[Microscopic Fungi|fungi]], insects, and worms.

> [!INFO] There's actually only two main decomposer types
> While the "four types" model is standard for exams, these are technically split into:
>
> - **Saprotrophs (External Digesters):** Bacteria and Fungi.
> - **Detritivores (Internal Digesters):** Invertebrates like worms and insects.

## The Process of Decay

When decomposers break down dead matter, the chemical reaction usually follows this pattern.
$$Organic\ matter + Oxygen \rightarrow Carbon\ dioxide + Water + Minerals$$

> [!INFO] How Compost Works
> Decomposers break down or decay your kitchen and garden waste into compost, a nutrient-rich soil that helps plants grow.
>
> Key Benefits:
>
> - **Nutrient Cycling:** Supplies nitrates, which plants absorb to synthesize amino acids (proteins) and chlorophyll.
> - **Soil Structure:** Improves water retention and filtration.
> - **Erosion Control:** Binds soil particles together, preventing nutrient runoff and erosion.
