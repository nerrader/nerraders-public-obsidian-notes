# Reproduction

Reproduction is one of [[The Seven Fundamental Life Processes]] carried out by an organism. It is a process where organisms produce new individuals known as offspring, inheriting characteristics from the parents via DNA.

This process allows the continuation of a species, which prevents it from extinction, and also allows for more unique variation in organisms.
## Types of Reproduction

There are two primary types of reproduction: sexual reproduction, and asexual reproduction:
### Sexual Reproduction

This process involves the combination of genetic material from two parents, usually one from each biological sex, typically by using specialized cells (called gametes). This results in unique offspring, providing diversity that aids in adaptation and survival.

However, there are some organisms that are hermaphroditic, meaning that an organism has both male and female parts. Hermaphroditic species may mate with others, fertilizing each other which produces more offspring, or they also may self-fertilize. Self-fertilization is quite common for organisms with low mobility, such as clams and barnacles.
### Asexual Reproduction

This process only revolves around one parent, and produces an organism that is genetically identical to the parent. This type of reproduction is usually found in prokaryotes like bacteria, and some eukaryotic organisms too.

There are more ways to reproduce asexually, like:

#### Budding

Budding is a form of asexual interaction, where a small part grows outward from the organism, and with enough time, leads to a separation from the original organism into two individuals. Budding occurs commonly in some invertebrates like hydras and corals.

![[budding example.png]]

#### Binary Fission

Binary Fission, also called fission, occurs in prokaryotic microorganisms and in some invertebrate, multi-celled organisms. After a period of growth, the organism separates into two smaller organisms.

![[binary fission diagram.png]]

#### Fragmentation

Fragmentation is the process of using a part of another organism's body to form a new separate body, and a new organism.

Sea stars reproduce primarily with this method, the cutting of a large part of the sea star will naturally reproduce and regenerate a new one.

![[sea star fragmentation example.png]]

Note that in fragmentation, the size of the two individuals can vary, unlike other methods like binary fission where it is usually identical.
