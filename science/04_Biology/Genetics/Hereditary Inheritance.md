Hereditary Inheritance refers to the study of how genetic information is passed down from parents to offspring.

There are three fundamental laws to Hereditary Inheritance:

## The Law of Dominance

In a pair of heterozygous alleles, the dominant allele masks the recessive allele, which also determines the organisms phenotype.
## The Law of Segregation

During gamete formation (meiosis), the two alleles for a trait separate so that one gamete only receives one allele.
## The Law of Independent Assortment

Alleles for different traits segregate independently of one another during gamete formation, provided the genes are on different [[Chromosomes]].

For example, consider pea plants with two traits:

- A round (R) seed shape is dominant over wrinkled (r).
- A yellow (Y) seed color is dominant over green (y).

A plant with the genotype **RrYy** can produce four types of gametes:

- Ry
- ry
- RY
- rY
