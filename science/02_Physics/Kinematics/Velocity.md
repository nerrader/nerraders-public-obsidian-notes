Velocity is a [[Types of Quantities#Vector Quantities|vector quantity]] that describes both the speed and direction of an object.

- The standard SI unit for velocity is $m/s$ + direction.
- Unlike speed, velocity measures displacement, not distance.$$velocity = \frac{displacement}{time}$$
Here’s an example that makes the difference between speed and velocity clearer:

Mark runs 10m west, then 10m east taking a total of 20 seconds.

So:

- **Speed** = total distance ÷ time = 20 m ÷ 20 s = **1 m/s**
- **Velocity** = displacement ÷ time = 0 m ÷ 20 s = **0 m/s**

As you can see, since it measures displacement, velocity is 0.

As you can see, because velocity is based on displacement, and since Mark ends up back at his starting point, his displacement is 0 m. Therefore, his average velocity is **0 m/s**, even though he was moving the entire time, while his speed remains at 1 m/s.
