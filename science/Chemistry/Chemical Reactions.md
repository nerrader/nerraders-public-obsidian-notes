Chemical reactions are a process where chemical substances (reactants) get transformed into different chemical substances (products).

This transformation usually involves breaking existing bonds and forming new bonds.
They do not create any new atoms, though the chemical structure is just rearranged.

This transformation is usually written down as a chemical formula.
## Conservation of Mass

In a closed system when a chemical reaction is happening, the law of conservation of mass states that atoms can not be created or destroyed, just transformed into different compounds and structures.
## Indicators

There are a few indicators to tell when a reaction is taking place:
- a new solid forming
- bubbles forming
- a color changing

## Types of Chemical Reactions

There are five main types of chemical reactions, with an additional one that I'll be talking about

### Combination

A combination reaction is when two reactants combine together to form a single product.
$$2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
This is usually caused because molecules or atoms or other compounds want to achieve a lower-energy state.
### Decomposition

A decomposition reaction is when a reactant decomposes/breaks down into two products.
$$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{SO}_3$$
This usually happens because energy is inputted that disrupts their chemical bonds.
### Single Displacement

A single displacement reaction is when an element replaces another in a compound.
$$\text{Zn} + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{ZnCl}_2 + \text{H}_2$$
These reactions happen because a more reactive element replaces another less reactive one.
In the example, zinc has higher reactivity than hydrogen, so it replaces hydrogen in $\text{HCl}$ and is rearranged into $\text{ZnCl}$.
### Double Displacement

A double displacement reaction is when two ionic compounds dissolved in water swap their positive and negative ions, forming two new compounds.
$$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{KOH} \to \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
Acid-base neutralization reactions are grouped into these types of reactions.

This reaction is driven forward by the formation of one of three specific products:

- **An Insoluble Precipitate**: Oppositely charged ions attract so strongly that they lock together as a solid, dropping out of solution.
- **A Neutral Covalent Molecule**: Ions combine to form a stable compound, such as water in an acid-base neutralization.
- **A Gas**: Ions react to form a gas (e.g., $\text{CO}_2$ or $\text{H}_2\text{S}$) that bubbles out of the solution.

If all possible ion pairs remain completely soluble, no product forms, and the ions simply remain floating in solution.
### [[Combustion]]

A combustion reaction is where a substance reacts with oxygen, releasing heat and light, typically producing water and carbon dioxide for hydrocarbons.
$$\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \to \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
Combustion is also considered an [[Oxidation]] reaction.
