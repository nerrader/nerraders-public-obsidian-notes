The **Periodic Table** displays all known chemical elements. It is organized by atomic number, electron configurations, and recurring chemical properties.

![[the periodic table.png]]

- There are 18 groups, aka 18 rows.
- Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons.
- Elements in a group share similar chemical properties.
  
- There are 7 periods, aka 7 columns.
- Elements in the same period have the same number of electron shells.
- Moving from left to right increases the atomic number by one.

## Element Information

- Atomic number, number of protons.
- Name of the elemnt
- Symbol
- Atomic Mass: Average mass number of the atom's isotopes

For more information, go to [[Atoms and Elements]].

## Types of Elements

There are three main types of elements in the periodic table: metals, semimetals, and nonmetals.

### Metals

Metals make up the vast majority of the periodic table. Their uniqueness makes it so that only metals may replace the function of other metals. Most metals also [[Oxidation|react with oxygen]], most halogens and other non-metals.

- Physical Properties: They are shiny, extremely dense, [[States of Matter#Solid|solid]] at room temperature, have high melting and boiling points, malleable, and ductile.
- Chemical Behavior: They easily lose electrons when they react with other elements.
- Metals are also good conductors of electricity and heat due to its high density.

### Semi-Metals

Semi-metals or metalloids form the "staircase" separating the metals from the non-metals, and their properties are a hybrid of both.

- Physical Properties: They usually look metallic and shiny, but they are brittle like non-metals.
- Electrical Properties: They are semiconductors. They don't conduct electricity as well as metals, but they do it way better than non-metals. You can also control how they conduct electricity by changing their temperature or mixing in tiny amounts of other elements. This is also the primary reason semiconductors are used instead of regular conductors.
- They can behave either like a metal or a non-metal depending on what they're reacting with.

### Non-Metals

Non-metals sit on the far right side of the periodic table (plus Hydrogen).

- Physical Properties: They are brittle when solid. Many non-metals are gases at room temperature.
- Electrical Properties: They are insulators.

## Periodic Trends

|                **Trend**                | **Across a Period →** | **Down a Group ↓** |
| :-------------------------------------: | :-------------------: | :----------------: |
|            **Atomic Radius**            |       Decreases       |     Increases      |
|          **Electron Affinity**          |       Increases       |     Decreases      |
| [[Electromagnetism\|Electronegativity]] |       Increases       |     Decreases      |
|          **Ionization Energy**          |       Increases       |     Decreases      |
![[periodic table trends.png]]

- [[Electromagnetism|Electronegativity:]] Atom's ability to attract and bind with electrons, quantified by the Pauling Scale.
- **Ionization Energy**: Energy required to remove an electron from a neutral atom in its gaseous phase. The lower this energy is, the more readily the atom becomes a cation.
- **Electron Affinity**: Ability of an atom to accept an electron.
- **Atomic Radius:** The atomic radius is one-half the distance between the nuclei of two atoms

## Important Group Families

Group families are basically the same thing as periodic table groups but just given a name.

- **Group 1 (Alkali Metals):** Highly reactive, they only have one valence electron.

- **Group 2 (Alkaline Earth Metals):** The less reactive version of the alkali metals, they have two valence electrons.
- **Groups 3-12 (Transition Metals):** The metals in the middle, their valence electron configurations are flexible meaning they have various charges.
- **Group 17 (Halogens):** Most reactive non metals.
- **Group 18 (Noble Gases)**: Very unreactive as they have a full outer shell.
