Chemical reactions can be classified by whether they release or absorb energy. There are two main types of energy changes: exothermic and endothermic.

- Exothermic reactions release energy into their surroundings, usually as heat. This usually makes the surroundings warmer.
- Endothermic reactions absorb energy from their surroundings, usually heat energy, which make the surroundings colder.

This depends on the energy involved in breaking and forming bonds:

- In exothermic reactions, more energy is released when new bonds form than is absorbed when old bonds break. This causes the leftover energy to be transferred into the surroundings.

- In endothermic reactions, more energy is absorbed when old bonds break than is released when new bonds form. The reaction then absorbs additional energy to form those new bonds from the surroundings.
