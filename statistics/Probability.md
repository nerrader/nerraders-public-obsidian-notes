Probability is the measure of how likely an event is to occur, and is measured from 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).

You might know this better as percentages:
- 0 = 0%
- 0.3 = 30%
- 0.45 = 45%
- 1 = 100%
You can also express these in fractions.

There are two types of probability, theoretical and experimental probability

## Theoretical Probability

Theoretical probability describes the probability of getting a favorable outcome, or based on what "should" happen. 

This is calculated by how many favorable outcomes there are divided by the total amount of possible outcomes that can happen, or sample space.

In equation form, its:
$$P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{n(S)}$$

## Experimental Probability

Experimental probability describes the probability based on results from an experiment.

This is calculated by the number of times the favorable outcome occurred, and the number of trials.

For example, if I rolled a die 20 times, and the number five was rolled twice, the experimental probability of rolling a 5 would be 10% or 0.1, even though the theoretical probability should be approximately 0.167 or $\frac{1}{6}$.

Though do note that the more trials you do, the experimental probability tends to go closer towards the theoretical probability
